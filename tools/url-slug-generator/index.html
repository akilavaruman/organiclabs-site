      Basics
      
What Is a URL Slug?

      
A URL slug is the last part of a URL that names the specific page.

      

        

          https://Protocol
          organiclabs.aiDomain
          /blog/Folder
          soc-2-compliance-softwareSlug
        

      

      
The domain and folder are usually set by your site. The slug is the part you choose for each page. It tells readers and search engines what the page is about before they open it. A good slug is short, lowercase, uses hyphens between words, and keeps the main keyword.

    


    

      SEO
      
Do URL Slugs Matter for SEO?

      
A small ranking signal, a bigger clarity signal.

      
Google says the ranking effect is small. John Mueller has said Google uses the words in a URL as a "very very lightweight factor", and that it matters even less once the content is indexed.

      
Google's URL structure guide still recommends simple, descriptive words over ID numbers, and hyphens over underscores.

      
The bigger benefit is clarity. People read slugs in shared links, Slack messages and emails. A slug shows the topic at a glance. And a clean, stable URL keeps every link, citation and bit of history pointing to one place.

    


    

      Rules
      
URL Slug Best Practices (The Rules This Tool Uses)

      
Seven rules. Give them to every writer.

      
These are the rules behind every option this slug generator gives you. They work for blog posts, landing pages and any other SEO-friendly URL you need.

      
    
        
  • Keep the keyword. It tells readers what they'll get.
    • 
        
  • Keep it short. Three to five words is enough for almost any page.
    • 
        
  • Use hyphens and lowercase. Mixed case can create duplicate URLs on some servers.
    • 
        
  • Drop years and list counts. They date the URL and force a redirect later.
    • 
        
  • Drop stop words unless you need them. Keep "vs" in comparisons.
    • 
        
  • Use plain ASCII. Accented letters turn into encoded strings like %C3%A7 when shared.
    • 
        
  • Keep folders shallow. One folder level is plenty.
    •

      Our Approach
      
Why We Drop the Year From Slugs

      
Update the page, not the URL.

      
Listicles and guides get refreshed every year. A year in the slug forces a choice when you update: keep a URL that looks stale, or change it and add a redirect.

      
Freshness matters more with AI search. Ahrefs analysed 17 million citations and found that AI assistants cite content that is 25.7% fresher on average than pages in Google's organic results. That held for ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Copilot; Google's AI Overviews were the exception. So keep one evergreen URL. Update the content and the visible date, and every link and citation keeps pointing to it.

      
Put the year in the title and H1 instead, where it can change freely.

      
A URL is a promise that the page will still be there. Don't date-stamp it.

      Changes
      
Should You Change an Existing URL Slug?

      
Usually not. Sometimes yes.

      
If the page ranks or has links, leave the slug alone. A slightly better slug rarely makes up for the disruption. Change it when the slug is an ID string, holds a year that now misleads readers, or has a typo.

      
If you do change it:

      
    
        
  • Add a 301 redirect from the old URL to the new one.
    • 
        
  • Update internal links to point at the new URL.
    • 
        
  • Update your sitemap.
    • 
        
  • Keep the old URL redirecting for the long term, not just a few months.
    • 
      

      
The "Changing a live URL?" toggle in the tool writes the redirect line for .htaccess, Nginx, Next.js and Webflow. Google's site move guide covers larger changes.

      FAQs
      
FAQs

      
What is a URL slug?
A URL slug is the last part of a URL that names a specific page, like "url-slug-generator" in organiclabs.ai/tools/url-slug-generator. Good slugs are short, lowercase, hyphenated and describe the page.

      
How long should a URL slug be?
Aim for 3 to 5 words, usually under 60 characters. Google doesn't set a limit, but short slugs are easier to read, share and remember.

      
Should I use hyphens or underscores in URLs?
Use hyphens. Google recommends hyphens over underscores because they help users and search engines identify separate words.

      
Should I include keywords in my URL slug?
Yes, include the main keyword, but don't stuff it. Google calls words in the URL a very lightweight factor, so the main benefit is clarity for readers.

      
Should I remove stop words from slugs?
Usually yes. Words like "the", "and" and "of" add length without meaning. Keep them only when the slug reads wrongly without them.

      
Should I put the year in a URL slug?
No. Put the year in the title instead. A year in the slug forces a redirect or a stale-looking URL when you update the page.

      
Is it bad to change a URL slug?
It can be. Changing a ranking URL resets some signals and breaks links unless you add a 301 redirect. Only change it when the new slug is clearly better.

Yes. No sign-up. It runs in your browser, including bulk mode, so your titles never leave your device.
